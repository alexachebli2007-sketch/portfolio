MY CONTRIBUTION

From research to product direction.

I explored scientific research and design considerations to identify potential product requirements and develop a proposed portable diffuser concept.

The concept was not manufactured or launched. The PRD, prioritization matrix, and validation framework presented later are retrospective portfolio extensions, not deliverables completed during the original project.

01

PROBLEM DISCOVERY

I didn't start with a product. I started with a problem.

THE QUESTION

How might we make stress management more accessible and easier to integrate into everyday life?

I began by exploring how everyday stress management could become more accessible and easier to integrate into people's routines.

My initial hypothesis was that portability, convenience, and ease of use could reduce friction in existing wellness experiences. This became the starting point for researching potential product directions.

EARLY INSIGHT

The opportunity wasn't simply to create another wellness product. It was to explore a solution that could be portable, accessible, and easy to use.

02

RESEARCH

I researched before
I designed.

I explored three areas to inform the product direction: the science of scent, biological mechanisms of fragrance dispersal, and sustainability considerations. Each area helped identify potential product requirements and design trade-offs.

01

Science

What could ylang-ylang contribute to the solution?

I investigated ylang-ylang and its use in aromatherapy, focusing on its volatile organic compounds and how scent interacts with the olfactory system.

PRODUCT IMPLICATION Explore scent as the core delivery mechanism.

02

Biomimicry

What could the flower teach me about delivery?

I examined how flowers naturally disperse scent and used that principle to think beyond continuous diffusion.

PRODUCT IMPLICATION Controlled, timed scent release to avoid over-saturation.

03

Sustainability

Could the solution work more responsibly?

I considered material choices and explored Damask rose as a more locally available alternative that could reduce transportation and environmental costs.

PRODUCT IMPLICATION Treat sourcing and materials as product decisions.

Investigating ylang-ylang, scent, and the olfactory system.

Studying natural scent dispersal to inform controlled release.

Exploring materials and local sourcing to reduce environmental impact.

03

PRODUCT CONCEPT

Turning research into a product direction.

THE DECISION

A portable essential-oil diffuser designed around accessibility, portability, and sustainability.

The goal was not simply to create another diffuser. I wanted the product format itself to address the constraints uncovered during research.

Portability could make the experience easier to integrate into everyday routines, while controlled scent delivery could create a more intentional user experience.

Defining the MVP.

Controlled scent release Must-have Make scent delivery intentional rather than continuously active.

04

PRODUCT DECISIONS

Every feature came with a trade-off.

I translated the research into product requirements, balancing user experience, feasibility, sustainability, and accessibility.

01

Portability

Fit the product into everyday routines.

PRODUCT CHOICE

Prioritize a compact, lightweight format rather than a traditional stationary diffuser.

02

Scent delivery

Avoid constant or excessive exposure.

PRODUCT CHOICE

Explore timed release and adjustable intensity so the user has greater control over the experience.

03

Materials

Balance durability with environmental impact.

PRODUCT CHOICE

Consider bamboo, recycled glass, and biodegradable plastics rather than treating sustainability as an afterthought.

04

Refillability

Reduce friction after the first use.

PRODUCT CHOICE

Design around simple refilling and cleaning to make repeat use easier for the customer.

05

Sourcing

Consider impact beyond the physical product.

PRODUCT CHOICE

Explore locally available alternatives where possible to reduce transportation and environmental costs.

PROPOSED ROADMAP

From concept to launch, one decision at a time.

The 8-week project established the product direction. I then mapped a proposed 12-month roadmap for how the concept could progress through validation, prototyping, testing, production, launch, and iteration.

01

MONTHS 1–3

Validate & Prototype

Interview potential users, test the core problem hypothesis, define MVP requirements, and develop an initial functional prototype.

OUTPUT Validated problem hypothesis and first prototype

02

MONTHS 4–6

Test & Refine

Conduct usability testing, evaluate scent delivery and refillability, and iterate based on user feedback and technical findings.

OUTPUT Tested prototype and revised requirements

03

MONTHS 7–9

Prepare to Launch

Assess manufacturing feasibility, sourcing, unit economics, pricing, and positioning before committing to production.

OUTPUT Launch feasibility assessment and go/no-go decision

04

MONTHS 10–12

Launch & Learn

If validation and feasibility milestones are met, run a limited pilot, measure adoption and satisfaction, and identify improvements.

OUTPUT Pilot results and next-iteration plan

PRODUCT MANAGEMENT ARTIFACT / 03

What would I validate first?

Before investing in development, I would test the assumptions behind the product concept and use the findings to inform the next iteration.

01 / DESIRABILITY

Do people want it?

Interview potential users about existing stress management routines and whether a portable diffuser addresses a meaningful need.

Evidence to collect Recurring needs, existing workarounds, and willingness to try the concept.
02 / USABILITY

Can people use it easily?

Test a low-fidelity prototype to observe how users understand scent controls, refilling, and everyday use.

Evidence to collect Task completion, points of confusion, and qualitative usability feedback.
03 / VIABILITY

Could it be sustainable as a business?

Explore willingness to pay, potential manufacturing costs, and trade-offs between materials, functionality, and price.

Evidence to collect Price sensitivity, preliminary cost estimates, and potential margins.

THE DECISION GATE

Validate the problem and demand before committing to a more expensive prototype.

This is a proposed validation framework. No user interviews, prototype tests, or commercial validation results are being claimed.

THE PRINCIPLE

Launch isn't the finish line. It's another source of information.

06

REFLECTION

What this project taught me about building products.

BIGGEST TAKEAWAY

A good product doesn't start with a good idea. It starts with understanding the right problem.

This project taught me how to move from an open-ended challenge to a structured product direction. I learned to connect scientific research, design considerations, and product decisions rather than treating them as separate activities.

It also highlighted an important limitation: research can inform a promising concept, but it cannot replace direct evidence of customer demand. Without user testing, the product's desirability and usability remain assumptions.

THREE LESSONS I'M TAKING FORWARD

01

Research should inform decisions.

The value of research lies in translating findings into product requirements and identifying what still needs validation.

02

Prioritization requires trade-offs.

Every proposed feature has implications for cost, complexity, usability, and sustainability. A focused MVP should test the most important assumptions first.

03

A concept is not a validated product.

The next stage would require user feedback, technical prototyping, and evidence that the solution addresses a meaningful need.

WHAT I WOULD DO NEXT

Move from research-informed assumptions to evidence-based product decisions.

My first priority would be to understand whether potential users actually experience the problem in the way I initially hypothesized. I would then test a low-fidelity prototype, evaluate usability, and use the findings to refine the product scope before committing to manufacturing.

LOOKING BACK / AN AI OPPORTUNITY

What if the product could adapt to its user?

One opportunity I'd revisit is scent intensity control. While basic dosage adjustments don't require AI, learning from user preferences could enable a more personalized experience.

My product takeaway: Explore AI where it adds genuine value, then validate demand, feasibility, and user trust before building.
