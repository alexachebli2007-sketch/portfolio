01

PROBLEM DISCOVERY

I didn't start with a product. I started with a problem.

THE QUESTION

How might we make stress management more accessible and easier to integrate into everyday life?

I began by exploring a broader challenge: modern lifestyles increasingly demand high cognitive performance while exposing people to persistent sources of stress.

I looked at existing approaches to stress management and considered where they created friction — from accessibility and convenience to the difficulty of integrating them into everyday routines.

EARLY INSIGHT

The opportunity wasn't simply to create another wellness product. It was to explore a solution that could be portable, accessible, and easy to use.

02

RESEARCH

I researched before
I designed.

Before defining the product, I explored the science, biomimicry, and sustainability considerations that could shape the solution.

01

Science

What could ylang-ylang contribute to the solution?

I investigated ylang-ylang and its use in aromatherapy, focusing on its volatile organic compounds and how scent interacts with the olfactory system.

PRODUCT IMPLICATION Explore scent as the core delivery mechanism.

02

Biomimicry

What could the flower teach me about delivery?

I examined how flowers naturally disperse scent and used that principle to think beyond continuous diffusion.

PRODUCT IMPLICATION Controlled, timed scent release to avoid over-saturation.

03

Sustainability

Could the solution work more responsibly?

I considered material choices and explored Damask rose as a more locally available alternative that could reduce transportation and environmental costs.

PRODUCT IMPLICATION Treat sourcing and materials as product decisions.

Investigating ylang-ylang, scent, and the olfactory system.

Studying natural scent dispersal to inform controlled release.

Exploring materials and local sourcing to reduce environmental impact.

RESEARCH → PRODUCT DECISION

The research didn't just support the idea. It changed what I designed.

03

PRODUCT CONCEPT

Turning research into a product direction.

THE DECISION

A portable essential-oil diffuser designed around accessibility, portability, and sustainability.

The goal was not simply to create another diffuser. I wanted the product format itself to address the constraints uncovered during research.

Portability could make the experience easier to integrate into everyday routines, while controlled scent delivery could create a more intentional user experience.

THE PRODUCT DIRECTION

A wellness product designed not only around what it delivers, but around how, where, and when people would use it.

04

PRODUCT DECISIONS

Every feature came with a trade-off.

I translated the research into product requirements, balancing user experience, feasibility, sustainability, and accessibility.

01

Portability

Fit the product into everyday routines.

PRODUCT CHOICE

Prioritize a compact, lightweight format rather than a traditional stationary diffuser.

02

Scent delivery

Avoid constant or excessive exposure.

PRODUCT CHOICE

Explore timed release and adjustable intensity so the user has greater control over the experience.

03

Materials

Balance durability with environmental impact.

PRODUCT CHOICE

Consider bamboo, recycled glass, and biodegradable plastics rather than treating sustainability as an afterthought.

04

Refillability

Reduce friction after the first use.

PRODUCT CHOICE

Design around simple refilling and cleaning to make repeat use easier for the customer.

05

Sourcing

Consider impact beyond the physical product.

PRODUCT CHOICE

Explore locally available alternatives where possible to reduce transportation and environmental costs.

PROPOSED ROADMAP

From concept to launch, one decision at a time.

The 8-week project established the product direction. I then mapped a proposed 12-month roadmap for how the concept could progress through validation, prototyping, testing, production, launch, and iteration.

01

MONTHS 1–3

Validate & Prototype

Refine the concept, define product requirements, develop early prototypes, and validate core assumptions.

OUTPUT Validated product direction

02

MONTHS 4–6

Test & Refine

Gather user feedback, evaluate usability, refine materials and functionality, and iterate on the design.

OUTPUT Refined prototype

03

MONTHS 7–9

Prepare to Launch

Finalize sourcing and manufacturing considerations while developing positioning, pricing, and launch strategy.

OUTPUT Launch-ready product

04

MONTHS 10–12

Launch & Learn

Introduce the product, measure performance and user response, and use those insights to guide iteration.

OUTPUT Insights for the next iteration

THE PRINCIPLE

Launch isn't the finish line. It's another source of information.

06

REFLECTION

What this project taught me about building products.

BIGGEST TAKEAWAY

A good product doesn't start with a good idea. It starts with understanding the right problem.

01

Research should change the product.

Research became valuable when it translated into decisions about functionality, sourcing, materials, and the user experience.

02

Constraints create priorities.

Portability, sustainability, affordability, and usability could not be considered independently. Each decision affected another.

03

A concept is only the beginning.

Mapping the proposed roadmap showed me how much validation, testing, iteration, and execution sit between an idea and a real product.

IF I CONTINUED THE PROJECT

My next priority would be validation.

Before investing further in development, I would validate the riskiest assumption first: whether people would actually integrate the product into their everyday routines. From there, I would test scent preferences, ease of use, willingness to pay, and repeat-use behavior.
